# RFC 3765: NOPEER Community for BGP

**Status:** Informational
**Date:** April 2004
**Authors:** G. Huston

## Overview

Defines the NOPEER well-known community (0xFFFFFF04). Routes tagged with
this community should not be advertised to bilateral peers. Complements
NO_EXPORT (not outside confederation) and NO_ADVERTISE (not to any peer)
from RFC 1997 by adding a finer-grained control: suppress redistribution
to peers while still allowing propagation to customers and upstream transit.

## Well-Known Community

| Community | Value | Hex |
|-----------|-------|-----|
| NOPEER | 4294967044 | 0xFFFFFF04 |

## Semantics

A route carrying NOPEER:

| Relationship | Advertise? |
|--------------|------------|
| Customer (downstream) | Yes |
| Transit (upstream) | Yes |
| Bilateral peer | No |

The RFC defines "peer" narrowly as a bilateral peering relationship
(settlement-free interconnection), not as a generic BGP neighbor.
Routes may still be sent to customers (who pay for transit) and to
upstream providers.

## Key Requirements

- Routes tagged NOPEER SHOULD NOT be redistributed to bilateral peers
- The community is transitive and propagated with the route
- A BGP speaker that does not understand NOPEER propagates it unchanged
  (standard behavior for unknown transitive communities)
- Does not affect route selection, only redistribution

## Use Cases

- Preventing route leaking at IXPs where bilateral peering occurs
- Allowing a multihomed customer to signal that certain prefixes should
  not traverse peering links
- Finer-grained control than NO_EXPORT when confederation boundaries
  do not align with peering boundaries

## Implementation Notes (Ze context)

- Constant `CommunityNoPeer` (0xFFFFFF04) defined in `attribute/community.go`
- Parsed as `nopeer` / `no-peer` / `no_peer` in `attribute/text.go`
- Displayed as `nopeer` in `attribute/text_append.go`
- The route server plugin (`bgp-rs`) does not currently enforce NOPEER
  semantics; the RS forwards to all clients regardless. This is correct
  per RFC 7947: the RS is not a peer in the bilateral sense.
