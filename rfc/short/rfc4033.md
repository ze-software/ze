## Purpose

Introduces the DNS Security Extensions: data origin authentication and data
integrity for DNS data, by signing RRsets (RRSIG) with zone keys (DNSKEY),
chaining zones through delegation signer records (DS), and authenticating
denial of existence (NSEC). It states what DNSSEC does not provide:
confidentiality, protection against denial of service, and protection of zone
transfers.

## Terms Ze relies on (§2)

| Term | Meaning | Ze |
|------|---------|----|
| Security-aware stub resolver | trusts a security-aware recursive server to validate, and understands the DNSSEC bits | ze's resolver (`dnssec-validation` permissive or strict) |
| Non-validating stub resolver | a security-aware stub that does not verify signatures itself | ze's resolver: it sets DO, leaves CD clear, and reads the upstream's verdict |
| Validating resolver | performs signature validation from a trust anchor | not ze |
| Trust anchor | a configured DNSKEY or DS RR hash | ze holds none |

## Stub resolver trust (§7)

A stub that places reliance on DNSSEC must trust both its recursive name servers
and the channel to them. RFC 4035 §4.9.3 states that obligation with RFC 2119
keywords, and `rfc/short/rfc4035.md` records ze's position on it (RFC4035-4.9.3-2,
the plain-UDP channel to the upstream).
