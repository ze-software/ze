# RFC 9012 corrections

Retired 2026-09-30: `RFC9012-13-7` remains the historical identity for §13, "If a Tunnel TLV has more than one of any of these sub-TLVs, all but the first occurrence of each such sub-TLV type MUST be disregarded." The retirement incorrectly treated the absent consumer as a different role. The RFC states a real obligation; absent implementation is a gap, not a reason to erase it. The current obligation is restored as `RFC9012-13-21`, with extraction site 13:7 mapped to that row. The retired identity is not reused. RFC9012-13-20 already owns the logging MAY; RFC9012-13-21 had no canonical or historical allocation in the inspected repository references.

The old claim that Ze reads none of the listed sub-TLVs is also false: `tunnelTLVLayout` and `tunnelEndpointValid` in `internal/component/bgp/reactor/session_tunnel_encap.go` count and validate Tunnel Egress Endpoints. This does not make duplicate endpoints a first-occurrence receive defect on Section 6 carriers. Section 3.1 says, "When the Tunnel Encapsulation attribute is carried in an UPDATE message of one of the AFI/SAFIs specified in this document (see the first paragraph of Section 6), each TLV MUST have one, and only one, Tunnel Egress Endpoint sub-TLV." Section 13 makes a TLV without exactly one endpoint a malformed-endpoint case; `applyTunnelEncap` removes it or invokes treat-as-withdraw when none survive. RFC9012-13-13/-14/-15 already account for this exception.

RFC9012-13-21 records the absent first-occurrence consumer for the other listed sub-TLVs. `TunnelTLV.SubTLVs` in `internal/core/bgp/attribute/tunnel_encap.go` retains every occurrence; its `Preference` accessor concerns RFC 9830 and is not proof of this obligation. RFC9012-13-8/-9 retain their separate not-malformed and propagation identities. The gap belongs to the already owner-selected `plan/spec-bgp-tunnel-encapsulation-consumption.md`. The RFC-compliance rule requiring an applicable absent behavior to remain a gap takes precedence over preserving a known false machine exclusion. Restoring the metadata does not authorize consumer implementation or grant conformance credit; sign-off dates, source quotes and derived records remain unchanged.

## 2026-10-08: clause scope and current receive behavior

- RFC9012-3.1-2/-3 now quote the Reserved-subfield antecedent and complete
  sentence. Their receipt-disregard and propagation identities remain distinct;
  the origination SHOULD remains RFC9012-3.1-1.
- RFC9012-3.2.1-1 names the reserved Flags bits rather than an unexplained
  “They”. VXLAN/NVGRE origination is still distinct from receive behavior.
- RFC9012-3.2.4-1 now cites GRE alone. New RFC9012-3.2.5-1 retains the separate
  MPLS-in-GRE sentence and its own extraction site. The tunnel names differ,
  so concatenating them into one supposedly identical quotation was wrong.
- RFC9012-3.7-2 retains the Prefix-SID sub-TLV and labeled-unicast antecedent.
  RFC9012-4.3-2 names the Color Extended Community Flags field; it is not the
  subsequent Color Value field.
- RFC9012-4.2-1 is an absent-consumer gap, not an impossibility: opaque
  Router's MAC and Encapsulation values can disagree without a decoder.
- RFC9012-11-7 quotes only its per-session outgoing-filter SHOULD. The
  preceding outgoing-filter MUST is already RFC9012-11-6.
- RFC9012-13-2 restores the final-octet condition. RFC9012-13-12 joins the
  line-wrapped sub-TLV spelling, and RFC9012-13-14 retains the complete endpoint
  exception. Derived extraction quotations remain untouched.
- The former “no endpoint validator” gaps on RFC9012-3.1-2/-4/-5/-7/-8 and
  RFC9012-13-13 no longer describe the producer. `tunnelTLVLayout` and
  `tunnelEndpointValid` now enforce framing, endpoint count/length and the
  embedded IANA destination/forwardable registry restrictions. Executed
  receive, storage and propagation checks do not replace fresh discrimination
  records or independent whole-row judgments.
- RFC9012-13-16/-17 remain blocked on the separately owned attribute-driven
  tunnel consumer. RFC9012-15-1 includes the duty of every participating router
  to filter traffic; attribute filtering is not that duty. These gaps and the
  MAC/Prefix-SID consumer conditions belong to
  `plan/spec-bgp-tunnel-encapsulation-consumption.md`, not to a fictitious
  dataplane inferred from preserved bytes. The optional §3.1.1 origin-AS
  procedure remains outside the commissioned work.
- Section 11's prior blanket statements now distinguish available mechanisms
  from missing defaults. `ingressStripCommunities` in
  `internal/component/bgp/plugins/filter_community/filter.go` removes configured
  exact values. `applyEgressFilter` in that package's `egress.go` and
  `genericCommunityHandler` in `handler.go` do the same on egress;
  `applySendCommunityMask` in
  `internal/component/bgp/reactor/peer_forward_facts.go` can suppress all extended
  communities. Those mechanisms do not justify saying no Encapsulation Extended
  Community can be filtered, but they do not prove the complete clauses.
- The former Pitfalls claim of implemented default EBGP filtering was false.
  Section 11 says, "For each external BGP (EBGP) session, filtering of the
  attribute on incoming UPDATEs MUST be enabled by default." It also says,
  "For each EBGP session, filtering of the attribute on outgoing UPDATEs MUST
  be enabled by default." For the Encapsulation Extended Community it says,
  "In both cases, this filtering MUST be enabled by default for EBGP sessions."
  `runIngressPolicyChain` and `runEgressPolicyChainASN4` in
  `internal/component/bgp/reactor/filter_ordered.go` accept without an active
  configured chain; `sendCommunitySuppression` supplies no suppression for an
  empty list or `all`. `textDeltaToModOps` in `filter_delta.go`, `attrNameToCode`
  in `filter_format.go`, and `attrModHandlersWithDefaults` in
  `filter_delta_handlers.go` supply no built-in attribute-23 scope filter.
  Endpoint validation is not administrative scoping. The summary retains these
  gaps rather than promoting the old Pitfalls assertion to conformance.
- RFC9012-13-17 now says header-construction behavior is "not proven by
  propagating opaque bytes". Section 13 says, "That is, they MUST NOT affect
  the creation of the encapsulation header." `checkRouteBestChange` in
  `internal/component/bgp/plugins/rib/rib_bestchange.go` publishes next hops,
  labeled-NLRI labels and SRv6 SIDs, not attribute-23 encapsulation parameters.
  No consumer implementation or conformance credit is added by this correction.
- Owner ruling 2026-10-08 removes only RFC9012-13-16's four obsolete
  positive/negative carry tags from
  `TestRFC9012TunnelEncapReceivedIgnoredAndPassedAlong` and
  `TestRFC9012MeaninglessSubTLVIgnoredNotRemoved`. Their assertions and
  RFC9012-13-18/-19 and RFC9830 coverage remain. The permanent 13-16 audit
  identity must be rejudged unimplemented with `checkRouteBestChange` as the
  actual Loc-RIB export boundary, not deleted or narrowed to partial carry.
- Focused configured Encapsulation Extended Community tests now supply
  `030c000000000002` and `030c000000000008` beside an unrelated Route Target
  through the registered community plugin, receive dispatch, cached export,
  socket output and downstream receipt. Populated no-match policies provide
  preservation controls. These new RFC9012-11-9/-10 tests still require
  execution, native binding and independent judgment; no enforced verdict or
  default-EBGP filtering claim is made here.
- RFC9012-13-10 now records positive-only polarity. Section 13 says the
  speaker "MUST process that TLV as if the unrecognized sub-TLV had not been
  present." An unknown type is legal input; the endpoint errors surrounding
  it belong to separate obligations. Existing carry and codec assertions
  remain, but their former negative 13-10 tags no longer misclassify that
  input. The new endpoint-processing carrier passed its 27 cases under race;
  a selective unknown-type early return then broke exact recipient bytes and
  installed-route removal while unknown-absent controls passed. All three
  claim roots passed after removing the overlay. Native bindings are recorded
  in `rfc/discrimination/rfc9012.json`, and independent whole-clause review
  rejudged 13-10 enforced. This grants no absent tunnel-selection or
  encapsulation-header consumer credit.

Correction 2026-10-09: `RFC9012-11-7` now has a two-destination proof of
the existing per-session outgoing attribute-23 filtering mechanism. One
received/cached route reaches both destination session writers and downstream
receive consumers. Only the configured destination loses attribute23;
swapping that destination preserves the other peer's exact attribute bytes.
Both tests pass twenty race iterations in `cba5d649`. Selective loss of the
raw override and leakage of one peer's stripping chain independently fail
the corresponding oracle while the opposite control passes in `2bf9854a`;
that receipt also records both native producer bindings.

This is unit-level production-path evidence using `recordingConn` and a
simulated external-plugin transport, not live daemon, TCP, FRR or dataplane
proof. The earlier blanket public statement that Section 11 filtering was
unimplemented was too broad. Default EBGP filtering remains absent, and
the per-session SHOULD proves neither that default nor the separate
attribute-driven tunnel consumer.
