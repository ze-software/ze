# RFC 7705: Autonomous System Migration Mechanisms and Their Effects on the BGP AS_PATH Attribute

## Meta

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| RFC | 7705 |
| Title | Autonomous System Migration Mechanisms and Their Effects on the BGP AS_PATH Attribute |
| Status | Standards Track |
| Date | November 2015 |
| Obsoletes | None |
| Updates | RFC 4271 |
| Obsoleted-by | None |

**Purpose:** Standardises the de facto vendor mechanisms ("Local AS", "No Prepend Inbound", "Replace Old AS", "Internal BGP AS Migration") that let a BGP speaker move from a legacy ASN to a permanently retained ASN without coordinating the change with its eBGP peers and without lengthening the AS_PATH those peers observe.

**Scope:** Session-level and AS_PATH-level behaviour of an existing BGP-4 speaker. No AFI/SAFI restriction is stated: the mechanisms are local to one speaker, require no negotiation, and apply to whatever families the session carries.

## Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| Legacy ASN (`<old_ASN>`) | The ASN being retired. In the document's examples, AS 64510 (ISP B) |
| Permanently retained ASN | The globally configured ASN kept after migration. In the examples, AS 64500 (ISP A, then ISP A') |
| "Local AS" | Per-neighbour instrument that supersedes the globally configured ASN in the "My Autonomous System" field of the OPEN, and by default prepends `<old_ASN>` to the AS_PATH of UPDATEs received from that neighbour (§3.1) |
| "No Prepend Inbound (of Local AS)" | Option that stops the `<old_ASN>` prepend on the inbound (CE to PE) direction (§3.1) |
| "Replace Old AS" | Option that stops the globally configured ASN being appended on the outbound (PE to CE) direction, leaving only the "Local AS" value (§3.2) |
| "Internal BGP AS Migration" | Per-neighbour instrument letting one iBGP session be formed with either the legacy or the permanently retained ASN in "My Autonomous System" (§4.1) |
| "BGP Alias" | Name §3.3 uses when pointing at the §4.2 model in which a speaker attempts the session with either of two ASNs |
| PE-A / PE-B | Provider Edge routers originally in ISP A and ISP B; PE-B is the one whose global ASN changed from 64510 to 64500 (§3.1) |
| CE-A / CE-B | Customer Edge routers peering with PE-A (AS 64499) and PE-B (AS 64496) (§3.1) |

The example ASNs 64496, 64499, 64500 and 64510 come from the RFC 5398 documentation range and are stand-ins for globally unique ASNs, not private-use ASNs (§1.2).

## Wire Formats

**This document defines no new message, path attribute, capability, NLRI or IANA code point.** It constrains how two fields that RFC 4271 already defines are populated. There is nothing new to encode or decode on the wire, which is precisely why the mechanisms need no negotiation and no cooperation from the neighbour (§1).

### Fields this document constrains

| Field | Carried in | Defined by | What this document changes |
|-------|-----------|------------|----------------------------|
| My Autonomous System | OPEN message, offset 1 in the OPEN body, 2 octets | RFC 4271 §4.2 | May carry a per-neighbour ASN different from the speaker's globally configured ASN (§3.1, §3.3, §4.2) |
| AS_PATH | UPDATE path attribute, type code 2, well-known mandatory | RFC 4271 §4.3, §5.1.2 | Which ASN(s) get appended, and in what order, when a session carries a "Local AS" (§3.3) |

Both the locally and the globally configured ASN may be four-octet, in which case the OPEN procedure varies per RFC 6793 §3 (§3.3, §4.2).

## Encoding Rules

### AS_PATH composition on a session carrying a "Local AS"

The AS_PATH is written leftmost-first, so the ASN appended last is the one nearest the receiving peer.

**Outbound (PE to eBGP neighbour), §3.3 item 2:** "The BGP router SHOULD first append the globally configured ASN to the AS_PATH immediately followed by the 'Local AS' value before advertising the UPDATE to an eBGP neighbor."

Appending the globally configured ASN first and the "Local AS" second puts the "Local AS" value outermost, closest to the peer that expects it, with the globally configured ASN immediately behind it. §3.2's worked result confirms the order: "By default, without the use of 'Replace Old AS', CE-B would see an AS_PATH of 64510 64500 64499", where 64510 is the "Local AS" (legacy), 64500 the globally configured ASN and 64499 the origin.

**Inbound (routes received from the eBGP neighbour), §3.3 item 1:** "The router SHOULD append the configured 'Local AS' ASN in the AS_PATH attribute before installing the route or advertising the UPDATE to an iBGP neighbor." The exact moment of the append is explicitly an implementation detail left outside the document's scope, with consistency of the AS_PATH across the AS and the loop-detection mechanism named as the considerations (§3.3).

### AS_PATH observed per mechanism combination

Inbound direction, routes originated by CE-B (AS 64496) and received by PE-B (globally 64500, "Local AS" 64510):

| Mechanisms on PE-B | AS_PATH inside ISP A' (Loc-RIB / to iBGP) | AS_PATH seen by CE-A | Source |
|--------------------|-------------------------------------------|----------------------|--------|
| None, PE-B still globally in 64510 (pre-migration) | 64496 | not stated | §3.1 |
| "Local AS" only | 64510 64496 | 64500 64510 64496 | §3.1 |
| "Local AS" + "No Prepend Inbound" | 64496 | 64500 64496 | §3.1 |

Outbound direction, routes originated by CE-A (AS 64499) and advertised by PE-B to CE-B:

| Mechanisms on PE-B | AS_PATH seen by CE-B | Source |
|--------------------|----------------------|--------|
| "Local AS" (+ "No Prepend Inbound"), no "Replace Old AS" | 64510 64500 64499 | §3.2 |
| "Local AS" + "Replace Old AS" | 64510 64499 | §3.2 |

The second outbound row restores the AS_PATH length to what it was before migration, which is the entire point of the mechanism (§3.2).

## Session Establishment

### External BGP ("Local AS"), §3.3

| Situation | Behaviour |
|-----------|-----------|
| "Local AS" configured for a neighbour | Send OPEN with the session's locally configured ASN in "My Autonomous System" (SHOULD), and do not send the globally configured ASN there (SHOULD NOT). The stated reason is to avoid making the eBGP neighbour "unnecessarily generate a BGP OPEN Error message 'Bad Peer AS'" |
| Flexible model (optional, "BGP Alias") | Attempt the session with either the "Local AS" ASN or the globally configured AS (MAY) |
| Flexible model succeeded on the globally configured ASN | The AS_PATH modifications of this document SHOULD NOT be performed: they are unnecessary, and the remote neighbour has effectively completed its own reconfiguration without coordination |
| Either ASN is four-octet | The procedure varies slightly, see RFC 6793 §3 |

### Internal BGP AS Migration, §4.2

| Situation | Behaviour |
|-----------|-----------|
| Received OPEN from a configured iBGP peer | MUST accept and establish when "My Autonomous System" is either the globally configured ASN or the locally configured ASN |
| Sending own OPEN | MUST send using either the globally configured or the locally configured ASN |
| Both ends configured with the mechanism | SHOULD send the globally configured ASN first and fall back to the locally configured ASN only after the neighbour answers "Bad Peer AS". This ordering exists to avoid a deadlock where both speakers pick the other's unexpected value |
| Session established either way | MUST treat UPDATEs sent and received as if the session were a natively configured iBGP session, per RFC 4271 and RFC 4456 |
| Either ASN is four-octet | The procedure varies slightly, see RFC 6793 §3 |

§4.1 states the semantic guarantee behind the last row: enabling "Internal BGP AS Migration" "preserves the semantics of a regular iBGP session (i.e., using identical ASNs)", so the attributes transmitted and the acceptable operations on attributes received are no different from an iBGP session without it.

## MUST Requirements

### Config

- "The mechanisms introduced in this section MUST be configurable on a per-neighbor or per-neighbor-group basis to allow for maximum flexibility." (§3.3, for "Local AS", "No Prepend Inbound" and "Replace Old AS")
- "The mechanism introduced in this section MUST be configurable on a per-neighbor or per-neighbor-group basis to allow for maximum flexibility." (§4.2, for "Internal BGP AS Migration")

A "neighbor group" is "a group of similar BGP neighbor statements that reuse some common configuration to simplify provisioning" (§3.3).

### Tx (AS_PATH the speaker emits)

- "No Prepend Inbound": "it MUST NOT append the 'Local AS' ASN value in the AS_PATH attribute when installing the route or advertising that UPDATE to iBGP neighbors" (§3.3)
- "No Prepend Inbound": "but it MUST still append the globally configured ASN as normal when advertising the UPDATE to other local eBGP neighbors (i.e., those natively peering with the globally configured ASN)" (§3.3)
- "Replace Old AS": "the BGP speaker MUST NOT append the globally configured ASN from the AS_PATH attribute" (§3.3)
- "Replace Old AS": "The BGP router MUST append only the configured 'Local AS' ASN value to the AS_PATH attribute before sending the BGP UPDATEs outbound to the eBGP neighbor." (§3.3)

### Rx / State (session establishment)

- "a BGP speaker MUST accept BGP OPEN and establish an iBGP session from configured iBGP peers if the ASN value in 'My Autonomous System' is either the globally configured ASN or a locally configured ASN provided when this capability is utilized" (§4.2)
- "a BGP router configured with this mechanism MUST send its own BGP OPEN [RFC4271] (see Section 4.2) using either the globally configured or the locally configured ASN in 'My Autonomous System'" (§4.2)

### Validation

- "In each case, the BGP speaker MUST treat UPDATEs sent and received to this peer as if this was a natively configured iBGP session, as defined by [RFC4271] and [RFC4456]." (§4.2)

### Errors

This document defines no new error code, subcode or NOTIFICATION. It references RFC 4271's existing OPEN Message Error subcode 2, "Bad Peer AS" (RFC 4271 §6.2), twice: once as the outcome the §3.3 SHOULD exists to avoid, and once as the trigger for the §4.2 fallback OPEN.

### Timers

None. This document defines no timer.

## SHOULD/MAY

- [SHOULD] "a BGP speaker SHOULD send BGP OPEN [RFC4271] (see Section 4.2) messages to the configured eBGP peer(s) using the local ASN configured for this session as the value sent in 'My Autonomous System'" (§3.3). Not doing so leaves the neighbour rejecting the session on the ASN it no longer expects.
- [SHOULD NOT] "The BGP router SHOULD NOT use the ASN configured globally within the BGP process as the value sent in 'My Autonomous System' in the OPEN message." (§3.3). Doing so makes the neighbour "unnecessarily generate a BGP OPEN Error message 'Bad Peer AS'".
- [SHOULD] "The router SHOULD append the configured 'Local AS' ASN in the AS_PATH attribute before installing the route or advertising the UPDATE to an iBGP neighbor." (§3.3). Skipping it makes the AS_PATH inconsistent across the AS and weakens loop detection.
- [SHOULD] "The BGP router SHOULD first append the globally configured ASN to the AS_PATH immediately followed by the 'Local AS' value before advertising the UPDATE to an eBGP neighbor." (§3.3). This is the ordering rule that puts the "Local AS" outermost.
- [SHOULD NOT] "If the session is successfully established to the globally configured ASN, then the modifications to AS_PATH described in this document SHOULD NOT be performed, as they are unnecessary." (§3.3). Performing them anyway lengthens the AS_PATH for no reason.
- [SHOULD] "the speaker SHOULD send BGP OPEN using the globally configured ASN first, and only send a BGP OPEN using the locally configured ASN as a fallback if the remote neighbor responds with the BGP error 'Bad Peer AS'" (§4.2). Ignoring the ordering risks a deadlock when both ends run the mechanism.
- [SHOULD] "BGP sessions SHOULD be protected using TCP Authentication Option [RFC5925] and the Generalized TTL Security Mechanism [RFC5082]" (§6).
- [MAY] "Implementations MAY support a more flexible model where the eBGP speaker attempts to open the BGP session using either the ASN configured as 'Local AS' or the globally configured AS as discussed in BGP Alias (Section 4.2)." (§3.3). Use it to let a remote neighbour reconfigure to the new ASN without any coordination.

## Algorithms

### Internal BGP AS Migration, order of operations (§4.1)

Step-by-step as the document gives it. Steps 1, 2 and 4 each cause an iBGP session reset and reconvergence on the devices touched.

```
1. In the legacy network, change ONE member of a redundant RR pair to the
   permanently retained ASN, and concurrently enable "Internal BGP AS Migration"
   on all of its iBGP sessions (Non-Client sessions to other RRs, and Client
   sessions to PEs). Redundancy absorbs the reset.

2. Repeat for the other RR of the pair, but REMOVE the mechanism from the
   Non-Client sessions toward the already-reconfigured RRs: it is no longer
   needed there. It is still required on every RR for all RR Client sessions.
   At the end of this step all RRs are globally on the retained ASN.

3. Establish iBGP sessions between all Route Reflectors in both the legacy and
   the permanently retained networks. The network now appears, internally and
   externally, as one consolidated network.

4. For each remaining legacy RR Client (PE): change its global ASN and its
   per-RR iBGP remote ASN to the retained ASN. Enabling "Internal BGP AS
   Migration" on those migrated sessions is unnecessary. In the same maintenance
   window, apply "Local AS", "No Prepend" and "Replace Old AS" to that PE's
   eBGP sessions, since all of the changes interrupt them anyway.

5. Remove the "Internal BGP AS Migration" configuration from the Route
   Reflectors in an orderly fashion. No vestige of the legacy ASN remains on
   any iBGP session.
```

Route Reflectors are used in the illustration only. §4.1 states the mechanism "can be enabled independent of the use of Route Reflectors".

## Pitfalls

### Edge cases

- **Loop-prevention hole from "No Prepend Inbound"** (§3.1): routers still in the legacy ASN can accept routes from which the legacy ASN was stripped "as if they have not already passed through their ASN, potentially causing a loop, since BGP's normal loop-prevention behavior of rejecting routes that include its ASN in the path will not catch these." The document's mitigation is careful filtering between routers remaining in the old ASN and routers migrated to the new one.
- **Outbound lengthening if "Replace Old AS" is omitted** (§3.2): in implementations where "Local AS" and "No Prepend Inbound" do not concurrently modify the outbound AS_PATH, the customer sees an extra ASN (64510 64500 64499 instead of 64510 64499). §3.2 calls this "an unacceptable increase in the AS_PATH length".
- **Four-octet ASNs** (§3.3, §4.2): the OPEN procedure varies slightly when the locally or globally configured ASN is four-octet, per RFC 6793 §3.
- **Deadlock when both ends alias** (§4.2): two speakers each configured with "Internal BGP AS Migration" can deadlock unless both try the globally configured ASN first.
- **Confederations** (§2): the document's use case explicitly assumes neither ISP uses BGP Confederations [RFC5065] internally.

### Interop

- The mechanisms are local to a single speaker, need no negotiation and no cooperation from the neighbour, and deployments do not need to interwork with one another, so "slight variations between existing vendor implementations exist and will not necessarily be harmonized due to this document" (§1).
- Existing implementations named in Appendix A: Cisco, Juniper, Alcatel-Lucent. The list is explicitly not exhaustive.
- Variations found in legacy router software that has not been upgraded are out of scope (§5).

### Operational lifetime

- External AS Migration mechanisms should be planned to persist indefinitely: an ISP does not control customer CE configuration, so "it is common that legacy ASNs and the associated External BGP AS Migration mechanisms discussed in this document can and do persist for several years, if not longer" (§5).
- Internal BGP AS Migration is under one administration and completes in days to months. §5 states it is good operational hygiene to avoid relying on it for a long period.

### Security

- Manipulating AS_PATH to keep its length constant is the whole mechanism, and getting it wrong shifts traffic: a lengthened AS_PATH typically loses the Path Selection Algorithm in other networks, "which could result in sudden and unexpected shifts in traffic patterns in the network, potentially resulting in congestion" (§6).
- The mechanisms are configuration-only and cannot be triggered remotely, so §6 asks for standard restriction of management-interface access, plus TCP-AO and GTSM on the BGP sessions themselves.

## Compatibility

### Behaviour with non-supporting peers

Nothing is negotiated, so a neighbour cannot support or fail to support these mechanisms. A neighbour peering with the legacy ASN sees an ordinary eBGP session with that ASN and is "completely unaware that ISP B has reconfigured its router to participate as a member of a new AS" (§3.1). What the neighbour does observe is a change to the CONTENTS of the AS_PATH, and, when the mechanisms are configured as the document describes, no change to its length (§2).

### Feature negotiation fallback

The only fallback defined is the §4.2 OPEN retry: globally configured ASN first, locally configured ASN only after "Bad Peer AS". The §3.3 "BGP Alias" MAY is the same idea applied to eBGP, and it carries the consequence that a session established on the globally configured ASN turns the AS_PATH modifications off.
