Enrolment

Enrolled: One MUST-level sentence (§3.2, customer-triggered RTBH): an operator who lets customers trigger blackholing MUST only accept announcements for prefixes the customer is authorized to advertise. RFC5635-3.2-1 restates the first condition RFC 7999 Section 3.3 states for the same customer-triggered blackhole, and Ze answers both through one producer (internal/component/bgp/plugins/rib/rib_blackhole.go::blackholeRouteType, which honours the blackhole only when coveredByAuthorized finds the prefix inside the peer's `blackhole prefixes`). The row is therefore a `{rollup}` onto RFC7999-3.3-1 and gates nothing of its own. The one SHOULD NOT (§4.1, source-based RTBH from peers or customers) is not gated. The walk is recorded in rfc/extraction/rfc5635.json at register rfc2119, its one site mapped. Enrolled 2026-10-26.

What the public ledger says

Status: Partial

What the ledger says is covered Customer-triggered destination RTBH is authorized per BGP session: Ze turns a received route into a discard route only when the prefix is covered by one of the peer's blackhole prefixes and the session named the blackhole community (internal/component/bgp/plugins/rib/rib_blackhole.go::blackholeRouteType), which is the Section 3.2 MUST, carried as a rollup onto RFC7999-3.3-1 and proven there. The discard route is a Linux FIB blackhole or a VPP drop path. Per-interface reverse path filtering, the uRPF half of source-based RTBH, is configured by Ze and performed by Linux (rp_filter for IPv4, an nftables fib rule for IPv6) or VPP.
What the ledger says remains No gated row of this document is open. The Section 4.1 SHOULD NOT (do not accept source-based RTBH announcements from peers or customers) has no dedicated guard: a blackhole is honoured on any session that named the community and authorized the prefix. Source-based RTBH as a whole (a discard route matched by the uRPF source lookup) has no end-to-end test.
