## Enrolment

Enrolled: UDP Encapsulation of IPsec ESP Packets (NAT-Traversal): eleven MUST-level requirements. Eight are met with tags in internal/component/ike: 2.1-1 (the ESP SPI is never zero), 2.1-3 (demultiplex on port 4500 -- a four-zero-byte marker is IKE, a single 0xFF byte is a NAT keepalive, otherwise ESP), 2.2-1 (the non-ESP marker of four zero bytes is prepended to IKE), 1-1 (a tunnel-mode client supports tunnel mode: every Child SA that negotiated no USE_TRANSPORT_MODE installs as tunnel, and a peer request the operator did not configure cannot change that), and 4-3 (a received NAT-keepalive reaches no SA, so it is never read as evidence the connection is live) carry positive+negative tags; 2.1-2 (the ESP SA's UDP-encapsulation ports are the ports the IKE SA uses: the peer's authenticated IKE port and this node's NAT-T socket port), 4-1 (NAT keepalives are sent at a conservative sub-binding-timeout interval) and 2.3-2 (the keepalive payload is one octet of 0xFF) are {single-polarity: positive} with new tests. The last three ids were extracted by the 2026-08-31 extraction sign-off, which found their sites unmapped. 3.1.2-1 (ESP-in-UDP encapsulation and transport-mode checksum fixup), 3.1.2-2 (inner checksum handling on decapsulation), and 2.1-4 (do not depend on a zero UDP checksum) are {not-applicable}: ze delegates UDP-ESP encapsulation and decapsulation to the kernel via XFRM_ENCAP_ESPINUDP and never inspects the UDP checksum.

## What the public ledger says

**Status:** Partial

**What the ledger says is covered:**

**What the ledger says remains**

None for Section 5.1 (`RFC3948-5.1-1`) among the Child SAs IKE peers negotiate: no two peers can hold a tunnel-mode Child SA and another Child SA, tunnel or transport mode, that one packet could match unless the operator configured and ordered both, and two transport-mode Child SAs of two peers are kept apart by the dataplane under Section 5.2 (`RFC3948-5.2-1`, `policyOwners.claim`), which the engine registry leaves them to. For a peer bound to a virtual-IP pool, the lease registry (`eap.Registry`, [`internal/core/eap/pool.go`](https://github.com/ze-software/ze/blob/main/internal/core/eap/pool.go)) holds each inner address for one configured peer at a time (Ze runs one session per configured peer), `assignVirtualIP` ([`internal/component/ike/engine/cp.go`](https://github.com/ze-software/ze/blob/main/internal/component/ike/engine/cp.go)) leases one on CP(CFG_REQUEST), and `substituteVirtualIP` makes every Child SA the peer asks for name the lease as its remote inner source, so two peers behind one NAT that both ask for 10.1.2.3 hold two distinct addresses (`TestTwoPeersSameRequestedAddressDistinctLeases`). A Child SA request Ze sends on that IKE SA, a rekey included, proposes the lease host as the peer's side (`proposeLeasedChildTSPayloads`), and `answerNamesOnlyLeases` refuses an answer naming the pool prefix or the wildcard there (`TestRFC3948ZeInitiatedRekeyNamesOnlyTheLease`). A bound peer holding no lease gets no Child SA. Every Child SA, tunnel or transport mode, pool-bound or not, is also held in the engine's cross-peer inner-selector registry (`innerSelectorRegistry`, [`internal/component/ike/engine/inner_selectors.go`](https://github.com/ze-software/ze/blob/main/internal/component/ike/engine/inner_selectors.go)): `narrowChildSelectorsIn` drops every narrowed pair one packet could match together with a live pair of another peer, keeping the initiator's first choice when it is free, and answers TS_UNACCEPTABLE when no pair is left, and `installChildSA` repeats the check and records the pairs under one lock before any dataplane call, so two peers bound to no pool that both propose 10.1.2.3 cannot both hold a tunnel for it (`TestRFC3948TwoUnboundPeersSameInnerAddressSecondRefused`, `TestRFC3948ConfiguredOverlapNarrowedByPeerRefused`, `TestRFC3948TwoUnboundPeersDistinctInnerAddressesServed`). The install-time check in `innerSelectorRegistry.claim` is the only guard on the paths the narrowing filter does not see: two responder sessions that both passed narrowing before either installed (`TestInnerSelectorInstallBackstopRefusesSecond`), and a Child SA this node installs as initiator from the responder's answer (`TestInnerSelectorInitiatorInstallRefusedInstallsNothing`). A record is released only once every policy its Child SA installed is gone (`removeChildSAExcept`, `releaseInnerRecord`), so a policy whose removal fails keeps its selectors reserved and the next overlapping peer refused (`TestRFC3948FailedPolicyRemovalKeepsRecord`), and a record stranded that way refuses an overlapping peer while every retry of the removal fails (`conflictLocked`, `TestRFC3948StrandedRecordRefusesOverlap`). A Child SA rekey carries the record to the replacement (`newRekeyedChild`), so retiring the old pair, whichever side started the rekey, leaves the peer's inner address held and the next peer proposing it refused (`TestRFC3948RekeyedChildKeepsInnerAddress`). Every IKE SA a configured session holds compares against the one registry its `SATable` owns: `startPeerSession` hands it to the session, `tryResponderSAInit` and `runInitiator` to the SA each one creates, and both IKE SA rekey directions to the replacement (`applyIKERekeyResponse`, `respondIKERekey`), so a second peer's overlapping proposal is refused at IKE_AUTH as responder and as initiator and in a CREATE_CHILD_SA after either IKE rekey (`TestRFC3948ResponderSessionsShareInnerRegistry`, `TestRFC3948ResponderSAInitRefusesHeldInnerAddress`, `TestRFC3948InitiatorSessionRefusesHeldInnerAddress`, `TestRFC3948ZeIKERekeyKeepsInnerRegistry`, `TestRFC3948PeerIKERekeyKeepsInnerRegistry`). An overlap stays legal only between pairs the operator wrote whole on both peers, each either a traffic selector configured on that peer and negotiated whole, both prefixes, the protocol and both ports, or, for a transport-mode pair, exactly the pair between the peer's configured `local-address` and `remote-address` for any protocol and any port on both halves, and only when the two peers' `policy-priority` values differ, so the SPD orders them (RFC 4301 Section 4.4.1, `operatorOrdered`); two such peers at one value, the default 2000 on both included, are unordered, and the second to establish is answered TS_UNACCEPTABLE (`TestRFC3948ConfiguredOverlapEqualPriorityRefused`). Distinct values order nothing a peer chose: a pair narrowed on either half inside the configured selector, or narrowed to one protocol or port between the configured addresses, is the peer's choice, judged on the policy it installs (its protocol is the intersection of the two halves'), and it is refused against any overlapping pair at any priority, whichever of the two arrives second (`TestRFC3948NarrowedByPeerDistinctPriorityRefused`, `TestRFC3948NarrowedProtocolDistinctPriorityRefused`, `TestRFC3948NarrowedPortDistinctPriorityRefused`, `TestRFC3948PeerChosenAgainstFixedDistinctPriorityRefused`, `TestRFC3948FixedAgainstPeerChosenDistinctPriorityRefused`), and a pair that differs from the configured selector only by its local port or only by its local prefix is the peer's choice too (`TestRFC3948ConfiguredSelectorNarrowedOnLocalPortRefused`, `TestRFC3948ConfiguredSelectorNarrowedOnLocalPrefixRefused`). A transport-mode Child SA is recorded too, so a tunnel another peer negotiates around a transport-mode peer's address, and a transport-mode connection inside another peer's tunnel, are refused in either arrival order, at narrowing and at install (`TestRFC3948TunnelAroundTransportPeerRefused`, `TestRFC3948TunnelAroundTransportPeerInstallBackstop`); `conflictLocked` leaves only the overlap of two transport-mode pairs to the dataplane. Distinct `policy-priority` values order a tunnel against a transport-mode pair exactly as two tunnels, when the tunnel is exactly a traffic selector its operator configured and the transport-mode pair is exactly such a selector or the pair between the peer's configured `local-address` and `remote-address` as host prefixes for any protocol and any port (`classifyInnerPair`, `configuredHostPair`, `isConfiguredHost`, `samePolicyPair`): a host-to-host transport-mode peer beside a full-tunnel peer configured with 0.0.0.0/0 is served at distinct values and refused at one, and refused at distinct values too when it narrows its proposal to TCP port 80 at its own address or at this node's, or proposes a host other than its configured address (`TestRFC3948OrderedTransportBesideFullTunnelServed`, `TestRFC3948OrderedTransportEqualPriorityRefused`, `TestRFC3948OrderedTransportNarrowedToPortRefused`, `TestRFC3948OrderedTransportLocalHalfNarrowedRefused`, `TestRFC3948TransportPeerChosenHostBesideConfiguredTunnelRefused`). The configured-host pair orders a transport-mode pair only: a tunnel peer with no traffic selector that proposes exactly the pair between its configured addresses beside a full-tunnel peer at a distinct value is the peer's choice, and whichever of the two arrives second is refused (`TestRFC3948TunnelPeerOwnHostPairBesideFullTunnelRefused`). Sixteen peers installing one inner address at once leave one recorded, because `claim` checks and records under one critical section (`TestRFC3948ConcurrentInstallsHoldOneInnerAddress`). Narrowing judges a Child SA rekey in the mode the rekey negotiates: transport only when the request carries USE_TRANSPORT_MODE and the retired Child SA runs transport, tunnel otherwise (`respondChildRekey`, `narrowChildSelectorsIn`), so a transport-mode Child SA rekeyed after an IKE SA rekey with the notify is judged as transport mode.

### [`RFC3948-3.1.2-1`](#rfc3948-3.1.2-1)

If the protocol header after the ESP header is a TCP/UDP header and the peer's real source and destination IP address have been received according to [RFC3947], incrementally recompute the TCP/UDP checksum: * Subtract the IP source address in the received packet from the checksum. * Add the real IP source address received via IKE to the checksum (obtained from the NAT-OA) * Subtract the IP destination address in the received packet from the checksum. * Add the real IP destination address received via IKE to the checksum (obtained from the NAT-OA). Note: If the received and real address are the same for a given address (e.g., say the source address), the operations cancel and don't need to be performed. 2. If the protocol header after the ESP header is a TCP/UDP header, recompute the checksum field in the TCP/UDP header. 3. If the protocol header after the ESP header is a UDP header, set the checksum field to zero in the UDP header.
