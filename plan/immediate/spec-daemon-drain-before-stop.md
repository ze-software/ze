## Task

Owner decision, 2026-10-13: "we need a drain phase for every protocol adding routes to the FIB/RIB" before the daemon stops.

Today a `ze` stop tears every routing protocol down at once: BGP sends Cease to every peer inside a 1 s budget, OSPF and IS-IS adjacencies drop when the plugin connection closes, and nothing tells the network to move traffic away first. Every neighbour loses its routes through Ze at the same instant and reconverges while traffic is black-holed. RFC 8326 (BGP graceful shutdown), RFC 6987 (OSPF MaxLinkMetric) and RFC 3787 (IS-IS overload) each describe how a router tells its neighbours to move traffic away before it leaves, and Ze performs none of them at stop.

Goal: a daemon-wide drain phase. After config changes are fenced and before any plugin is torn down, the daemon sends one `drain` RPC, in parallel, to every plugin that registered for it through the SDK. The RPC carries the stop kind (`stop` or `restart`), and each plugin decides what that kind means for its protocol. Each protocol makes itself unattractive (BGP GRACEFUL_SHUTDOWN re-advertisement of the paths RFC 8326 Section 4.2 covers: every path sent over an eBGP session, and the originated and eBGP-learned paths sent to iBGP peers, those with LOCAL_PREF 0; OSPF max-metric; IS-IS overload), waits its configured time, and answers. The daemon bounds the phase with a ceiling counted from the start of the stop and kept below the supervisor's kill timeout, refuses a budget or a configured wait above that ceiling, and lets a signal received during the stop abort the drain (a later signal forces exit). On `restart`, BGP answers at once and takes the graceful-restart path (RFC 4724 Section 5).

`plan/immediate/spec-ospf-max-metric-startup-shutdown-windows.md` depends on this spec: its Q-1 (where the `on-shutdown` window runs) is answered by this spec's drain phase, and its interim refusal of `on-shutdown` above 0 (its AC-11) is replaced by AC-11 here. Q-6 deletes that leaf, `ospf max-metric router-lsa on-shutdown`, and replaces it with `ospf drain-on-shutdown`. That spec's rows naming the old leaf were repointed to the new one on 2026-10-13, citing this Q-6; its interim refusal keeps its meaning under the new name, and whichever of the two specs is implemented first performs the rename (Files to Modify, "OSPF rename").

**Key insights:**
- There is no point today where all plugins are connected and the daemon waits for protocol work: every stop budget is 3 s or less, BGP's Cease budget is 1 s.
- `request shutdown` and SSH `stop` stop the BGP reactor (Cease) BEFORE the hub's ordered teardown starts, so a drain in the hub alone would run after BGP already left. Both paths must route through the hub.
- `request shutdown` is not the only handler that calls `r.Stop()` first: `handleDaemonQuit` does too. `stopDaemonForPlugin` (plugin failure policy) already goes through `shutdownFunc`, so it reaches the hub (I-6).
- Restart/reboot already stopped the reactor silently before the hub runs; the hub needs to know the stop kind and passes it in the drain RPC.
- Three supervisors deliver the stop SIGTERM to more than ze: systemd's default `KillMode=control-group` signals every process in the cgroup, the gokrazy supervisor signals ze's whole process group (`syscall.Kill(-pid)`, `service.Signal` in the pinned module), and a terminal Ctrl+C signals the foreground process group. External plugins are spawned by `exec.CommandContext` through `sh -c` with no `Setpgid`, so they share ze's process group, and they stop on SIGTERM through `sdk.SignalContext`: every one of them dies at the start of the drain. The fix is two parts: external plugins get their own process group, and the unit gets `KillMode=mixed` (the cgroup is not a process group) (I-3).
- The route-source registry is `redistevents.RegisterProtocol`; MPLS label sources (LDP, RSVP-TE) and VRRP addresses are outside it, and so are several direct kernel-route installers (table below).
- The BGP egress rails are more than two, and the drain tag must reach all of them by construction, not by inspection (B-3, Current Behavior). They run two different egress chains, `r.orderedEgressSteps` (`forwardUpdateCore` and every rail built on it) and `r.egressFilters` (the route server only), so the drain step sits in both (round 3, B-1).
- The drain walk and the writes in flight reach bgp-rib on different paths, so their order is made by a barrier, not assumed (round 3, B-2).
- The tag decision needs to know where each path came from (originated, learned from eBGP, learned from iBGP), and the rails do not all carry that today: the route-server rail's source has no LocalAS, the LLGR stale rail passes a zero source, and the originated-route gate `exportFilterForBody` receives no source at all. Each rail's provenance is named in Data Flow 5 (round 2, B-2), taken from `PeerSettings.IsIBGP` on the forward rails and, at the gate, from facts the reactor already holds under `writeMu`: the writer's owner item `Session.sentForward` and the destination's adj-out owner of the body's NLRI (round 3, N-1, I-1; round 4, I-11, which removed the provenance channel from bgp-rib).
- The reactor's Adj-RIB-Out keeps ownership and signatures, not attributes, so it cannot be replayed. The only per-peer copy of what was sent, with attributes and provenance, is bgp-rib's `ribOut` (round 2, B-1).

**Behavior to preserve:**
- Restart/reboot (`request reboot`, SSH `restart`/`reboot`): GR marker written, BGP sessions dropped in silence, no Cease, no GRACEFUL_SHUTDOWN re-advertisement. The drain RPC carries kind `restart`; BGP answers it at once.
- After the drain, BGP still sends Cease / Administrative Shutdown within `shutdownNotifyBudget`.
- `flush-on-stop` false keeps kernel routes after stop.
- A commit during shutdown is refused with `errDaemonStopping`.
- `forceExitOnSignal` runs on its own channel that a `request shutdown`-injected SIGTERM or a SIGHUP does not trip.
- With no plugin configured to drain (all waits 0), stop time does not grow.

**Behavior to change:**
- New drain phase between `commitReloads.close()` and `gnmiSrv.Stop()`.
- `request shutdown`, `quit` (`handleDaemonQuit`), SSH `stop`, and the CLI shutdown function stop BGP through the hub (drain, then teardown), never by calling `r.Stop()` first.
- Signals after the stop begins follow the state table in Data Flow: a signal before the drain has finished skips or aborts the drain and the ordered teardown continues; a signal after that forces exit 1 as today. An operator facing a hung config fence therefore needs one signal more than today to force exit: the first skips the drain, the next forces (round 2, N-6).
- The single line `run` prints after `commitReloads.close()`, "Shutting down (Ctrl+C again to force)...", is false during the fence and the drain, where the next Ctrl+C skips or aborts the drain. It becomes one line per phase (signal state table, Data Flow) (round 2, N-6).
- The drain deadline is counted from the start of the stop, so a slow config fence spends from the ceiling.
- `eng.Stop` gets its own context, created after `apiServer.Stop()` returns.
- External plugin subprocesses start in their own process group; unit gains `TimeoutStopSec` and `KillMode=mixed`.
- OSPF `drain-on-shutdown`, new BGP and IS-IS waits originate their drain, and drain state is an input to every later origination until stop.

## Data Flow (MANDATORY - see `ai/rules/architecture.md`)

### Entry Point
| Entry | Today | Stop kind | Drain |
|-------|-------|-----------|-------|
| SIGTERM/SIGINT from systemd, gokrazy or a terminal | `stopCh` | `stop` | yes |
| `request shutdown` (`handleDaemonShutdown`) | `r.Stop()` first, then signal | `stop` | yes, routed through the hub |
| `quit` (`handleDaemonQuit`) | stack dump, `r.Stop()` first, then signal | `stop` | yes, routed through the hub after the stack dump (the dump is its only difference from shutdown) |

- Config: per-protocol wait leaves, verified at commit against the plugin's declared `DrainBudget`; Stage 1 `DrainBudget` verified against the daemon ceiling.

### Transformation Path
1. Stage 1: a plugin with an `OnDrain` handler declares `DrainBudget` (seconds) in its registration; `startup.go` refuses a budget above the ceiling or a budget without a handler.
2. Commit verify: each protocol's verify handler refuses its wait leaf above its declared budget.
3. Stop: `waitLoop` returns and the hub records the stop start time; it fences config (reload worker, confirm window, `commitReloads.close()`), reads the stop kind, and calls the plugin server's drain fan-out with a context whose deadline is the earlier of (stop start + ceiling) and (drain start + largest declared budget), cancelled by a signal per the state table below. A fence that has already spent the ceiling leaves the drain no time: the hub logs "drain skipped: stop deadline passed during config fence" and continues.
4. Fan-out: one `drain` RPC per drain-registered plugin, all at once, each carrying the stop kind, on kind `restart` its cause (`in-place` or `reboot`, round 4 I-8), and the deadline (no other field: round 9, I-5); the server collects replies and logs late or failed plugins by name.
5. Plugin: the SDK hands the drain to its own goroutine, so the plugin keeps answering commands and RPCs (B-2). On kind `restart` BGP replies at once. On kind `stop`:
   - BGP (redesigned in round 2, B-1, B-2, I-1, I-9). The handler runs these steps in order:
     1. It checks that a COMMUNITIES attribute-mod handler is registered (`filter_community` registers it; LOCAL_PREF uses the generic handler, N-5). Without one the community cannot be added on any rail, so it logs the refusal "bgp drain refused: no COMMUNITIES attribute handler is registered, GRACEFUL_SHUTDOWN cannot be added", replies at once, and the Cease follows as today.
     2. It sets the reactor's drain flag, one atomic value owned by the reactor the BGP plugin runs. From this point every UPDATE written passes the drain verdict below.
     3. It makes every write that preceded the flag visible to bgp-rib before the walk (round 3, B-2: sent events and the replay command reach bgp-rib on two different paths, so the order is made, not assumed). In order, under the drain context, holding no lock across a wait (`DrainEventsApplied`: "Callers MUST NOT hold locks needed by plugin handlers while waiting"):
        1. It resolves bgp-rib's process from the dispatcher registry by the drain-replay command's name, `request bgp rib drain-replay` (a hidden bgp-rib command, declared in `rib.go`'s `decls` beside `request bgp rib recovery` with `Hidden: true`, handled from a row of the `rib_commands.go` handler table, and kept out of `ze-rib-api.yang` as `request bgp rib recovery` is, because no operator calls it; round 4, I-9), the way `relay_recovery.go` resolves "request bgp rib recovery". A nil command or process is the "no Adj-RIB-Out replay" refusal below.
        2. For every Established peer it takes and releases `Session.writeMu` once: a per-peer `Session.writeMu` take-and-release, which the drain performs itself and which reads no send sequence (the rs-replacement spec deletes `recoverySnapshot` and `Peer.sentUpdateSequence` with its destination-wide fence): every write that completed before the flag, gated or forwarded, has then enqueued its sent event on bgp-rib's `eventChan`.
        3. It calls `DrainEventsApplied` on bgp-rib's process: a `Barrier` delivery through the same per-process FIFO as the sent events, which returns once bgp-rib has applied every event admitted before it, so `ribOut` holds every path written before the flag.
        4. It dispatches the drain replay: `request bgp rib drain-replay` runs the `outboundResend` walk over every up peer, and each replay command carries the existing `replay` meta key with the value `drain` instead of `true`, beside the sent-owner receipt (round 4, N-2: no new key and no new field). bgp-rib's `ribOut` is the replay source for both populations, forwarded and originated, because it is the only per-peer copy of what was sent that holds attributes (Current Behavior). The walk collects every peer's groups under `peerMu` held for writing, not the `RLock` `outboundResend` takes today, and sets bgp-rib's "drain snapshot taken" state inside that same section (round 4, N-7).
        One population remains: a forwarded body computed on a forward rail before the flag (its drain step saw the flag clear, so it is untagged) and written after it. Its sent event can reach bgp-rib after the walk's snapshot. The mark is per write and never touches the shared `fwdItem.meta` map (round 4, I-1): inside the `writeMu` section that performs the write, the writer loads the drain flag and assigns a session field beside `sentMeta`: set when the flag is set and the write was not judged in drain mode, clear otherwise. The writer assigns it, set or clear, for every item at the point where it assigns `sentMeta` for that item (`session.sentMeta = item.meta` in `forward_pool.go`, the same assignment in `tryDirectWriteNoFlush`), and the batch-end defer that clears `sentMeta` clears it too, so a mark never survives from one item to the next one in a batch (round 5, I-3). The merge happens at the sent-event call in `Session.writeUpdateBody` (`session_write.go`), under `writeMu`: when the field is set, that call passes a fresh copy of `s.sentMeta` with the `drain-unjudged` key added (`maps.Copy`, the way `reactor_notify.go` adds `config-static`), and otherwise passes `s.sentMeta` unchanged. `notifyMessageReceiver` cannot do it, because it receives the meta as an argument and must not read the session's `writeMu`-guarded fields (Current Behavior, round 5, I-2). A flag load before `writeMu` is taken would let an unmarked write land after the snapshot, so the load is inside the section (round 4, I-2). "Judged in drain mode" means judged with the flag set by the forward drain step or by the gate (round 4, I-3): the writer marks only pre-filtered writes, which are an item's `rawBodies` and the `updates` of an item that is not `originated`, and only when the item's judged bit (`fwdItem` gains it, set by the drain step when it observed the flag) is clear; `fwdBucketMerge` (`forward_bucket.go`) copies the bit into every merged item and adds it to its run condition, so a merge never joins a judged item with an unjudged one and never drops the bit (round 5, N-2); an `originated` item's `updates` go through `writeUpdateGated(update, true)` and are judged by the gate at write time, so they carry no mark. Both writers of a pre-filtered forward apply this rule: the forward worker's batch handler (`forward_pool.go`) and the route-server direct path `tryDirectWriteNoFlush` (`forward_rs.go`), which computes its verdict before it takes `writeMu` (round 4, I-2). Every such write is marked, announcement or not: the writer does not parse the body (one raw body can carry NLRI and withdrawals), and bgp-rib replays only the NLRI it recorded from that event, so a withdrawal records nothing to replay (round 4, I-1). bgp-rib's `handleSent`, inside the `peerMu` section in which it records the event, reads the "drain snapshot taken" state, and when it is set and the event carries `drain-unjudged`, queues a one-entry `replay: drain` command to that peer, after recording the event in `ribOut`; it dispatches the queued command after `peerMu` is released, never under it, because the command is an engine call with its own timeout (`updateRouteWithMeta`) and a dispatch under `peerMu` would stall every bgp-rib handler until it returns; this is the collect-then-dispatch shape `rib_commands.go` already uses (`withdrawRemovedSentLocked`, unlock, `dispatchSentLifecycle`) (round 5, N-1). The self test is needed because `floodLink` also floods Grace-LSAs this router did not originate (round 8, I-1): `LSDB.Tick` (`lsdb/aging.go`) marks every link-store entry that reaches MaxAge as purged, whoever originated it, and calls `floodLink` for it, so a neighbour's Grace-LSA held here as helper and never flushed by that neighbour (it crashed mid-restart, or failed its exit) is flooded under the other router's Grace key, and `isGraceLSA` reads only the type and opaque type, never the advertising router. An entry is replaced by the next flood of that key to that neighbour and is never deleted by an acknowledgement, an interface teardown (`ReleaseLink` deletes `retransmit` lists and the per-interface stores, not this map, so the map outlives it) or `removeFromAllRetransmit`, so a pair that acknowledged, or lost its adjacency, before the wait set is built still reads as queued. The map is bounded by the neighbour keys flood-eligible at some self Grace flood times the self Grace keys flooded on their interface: on v2 one Grace key per neighbour key (the Type-9 opaque type 3 key, the same on every interface), on v3 one per interface ID the interface has carried, each under every router ID this process has held. A self Grace key is flooded by the restart paths and by its own MaxAge flush, and each flood replaces the entry, so the map never holds more than one header per neighbour key and Grace key. A new exported query, `LSDB.GraceQueued(nbr NeighborKey, key types.LSAKey) (packet.LSAHeader, bool)` in `lsdb/flooding.go`, reads it under `d.mu`.
     3. Once `prepareRestart` returns, a Full pair joins the wait set only when its interface's result holds an instance and `GraceQueued` holds that same instance for it (`sameInstance`, `lsdb/flooding.go`). Every other Full pair is logged "ospf drain: Grace-LSA not queued for neighbour" with the interface and router ID, and is never counted acknowledged: each Full neighbour of a refused interface, and each Full neighbour with no matching record.

     "Not in the set" therefore means not queued by this origination, and "in the set and no longer pending" means acknowledged or lost, decided below. The producers queue nothing for a Full neighbour in these cases: `OriginateLinkSelf` installs and floods nothing, and returns `ok` false, when the existing self Grace-LSA has the same body (`existingLinkSelfBodyUnchanged`) or `nextLinkOwnSequence` refuses inside MinLSInterval, on v2 through `OriginateOpaque` and on v3 through `v6OriginateGraceLSA`, so the interface's result is refused; v2 `floodLink` skips a neighbour that is not opaque-capable for the Type-9 Grace-LSA (RFC 5250 Section 3.1); `queueRetransmit` returns false once the neighbour's list holds `MaxRetransmitPerNeighbor` entries; `floodLink` returns before its loop when the interface is missing from its topology snapshot or the entry is gone; and a neighbour that was not flood-eligible at the flood and reached Full afterwards gets the Grace-LSA the link LSDB holds through database exchange, with nothing queued. Each would otherwise read as acknowledged at once, the false acknowledgement RFC 3623 Section 2.1 (below) makes the wait exist for. Rejected, round 6's query of the retransmission lists after `grOriginateGraceLSAs` returns: `floodLink` sends the LSU in the same call, the LSAck is handled on the receive goroutine (`clearRetransmit`, under `d.mu`), and the origination goes on to the next interface, so a neighbour that acknowledged inside that window read as not pending and was logged "not queued", a false operator line. Also rejected, returning the queued neighbour keys up through `floodLink`, `installLinkOriginated` and `OriginateLinkSelf`: every Link-LSA origination takes that path, so it changes three shared signatures and builds a slice per flood, where the map is written only for a Grace key. A pair is acknowledged when a new exported LSDB query, `LSDB.RetransmitPending(nbr NeighborKey, key types.LSAKey) bool` in `lsdb/flooding.go`, read under `d.mu` beside the unexported `queueRetransmit` and `clearRetransmit`, answers that the interface's Grace-LSA is no longer on that neighbour key's retransmission list, and the neighbour is still Full on that interface (`hasFullNeighbor(iface, router)`). The Grace-LSA key per interface is the key of the instance the interface's result holds (the header's own key, which `installLinkLocked` stores under and `queueRetransmit` keys by), so the wait derives no key itself, on either version: the v2 key helper round 6 (N-2) planned, and the wait's reuse of v3's `v6SummaryLSID(InterfaceID)`, are dropped (round 7, I-2), and a test pins the result's key to the key `OriginateOpaque` installs on v2 and `v6OriginateGraceLSA` installs on v3. Absence from the list alone is not acknowledgement: an interface teardown deletes the lists of every neighbour on it (`lsdb/link_scope.go`), and `removeFromAllRetransmit` drops a key from every list, so a neighbour that leaves Full before its list empties is logged "ospf drain: adjacency lost before Grace-LSA acknowledgement" with the interface and router ID, and is not counted acknowledged. A neighbour that leaves Full and is Full again by the next check has had its list deleted at teardown, so it reads as not pending and Full and is counted acknowledged without an LSAck. The outcome holds, because the new adjacency's database exchange gives it the Grace-LSA the link LSDB still holds, so "acknowledged" in the drain's figures means acknowledged or resynchronised (round 7, N-2). A third route empties a list with no LSAck and no teardown (round 8, N-3): a Newer receipt of a link LSA calls `removeFromAllRetransmit(area, key)` (`ReceiveUpdate`'s LinkLocal branch, `lsdb/flooding.go`), and while `selfFlushSuppressed()` holds, which it does from the moment `prepareRestart` raises `gracefulStop` (`instance.go`, `SetSelfFlushSuppress`), `handleSelfLinkReceived` (`lsdb/link_scope.go`) returns false, so a neighbour's newer copy of our own Grace key is installed and the key leaves every list. Every Full neighbour on that interface then reads as acknowledged without an LSAck. The case needs a neighbour holding a higher sequence of our Grace key than this origination, which is rare once `deletePurgedLinkIfAcked` has reset `linkOwn`, and in it the neighbours hold a Grace-LSA under our key, though its body is the one the newer copy carries and not this origination's. The spec states the case and does not close it. Rejected, counting acknowledged only on a recorded `clearRetransmit` of the matching instance: the round-7 Grace map holds only the queued header, so marking an acknowledgement adds a field to its value and a new query in place of `RetransmitPending`, and it would also stop counting the resynchronised neighbour above, whose list is deleted at teardown with no `clearRetransmit`, so that pair would wait for the deadline. The wait ends when every pair is acknowledged or lost, or when the drain context ends, which logs each unacknowledged pair by interface and router ID (round 4, I-7; round 5, I-7). OSPFv3 has no LSDB of its own, so the one query serves both versions. Replying once the Grace-LSAs are queued is not enough: the reply lets teardown close OSPF's sockets at once, so a Grace-LSA dropped or not yet sent is never retransmitted. RFC 3623 Section 2.1: "If Router X wants to ensure that its neighbors receive the grace-LSAs, it should retransmit the grace-LSAs until they are acknowledged (i.e., perform standard OSPF reliable flooding of the grace-LSAs). If one or more fully adjacent neighbors do not receive grace-LSAs, they will more than likely cause premature termination of the graceful restart procedure (see Section 4)." The grace reason is chosen per entry point (round 4, I-8). The drain RPC carries, beside kind `restart`, a restart cause with two values the hub knows without naming a protocol: `in-place` (SSH `restart`) and `reboot` (`request reboot` and SSH `reboot`, the two that set `rebootRequested`). OSPF maps `in-place` to 1 (software restart) and `reboot` to 2 (software reload/upgrade), the value `request ospf graceful-restart` already uses (`grPrepare`, `grReasonReload`). RFC 3623 Appendix A: "Encodes the reason for the router restart as one of the following: 0 (unknown), 1 (software restart), 2 (software reload/upgrade) or 3 (switch to redundant control processor)." The value matters to a helper: RFC 3623 Section 3.1 lists as example policies "a) never act as helper, b) never allow the grace period to exceed a Time T, c) only help on software reloads/upgrades, or d) never act as a helper for specific routers (specified by OSPF Router ID)", so a helper under policy (c) refuses reason 1, which is why the reboot entry points do not send it; answering at once is not enough, because nothing but `prepareRestart` starts a planned restart, and its only caller today is the operator command `request ospf graceful-restart` (`grPrepare`), so without the call the adjacencies drop with no Grace-LSA and the neighbours flush Ze (round 3, I-4). OSPF with the helper role only, or with no graceful restart, drains as on `stop`; IS-IS, which has no graceful restart in Ze, drains as on `stop`. BGP is already stopped in silence on this kind (`stopForRestart`), so an IGP wait spends from the BGP peers' restart time (R-12).
6. Hub continues the existing teardown: gNMI, API shutdowns, `apiServer.Stop()` (BGP Cease), then `eng.Stop` with its own context.

**Signal state table** (I-4). "Signal" means an OS SIGINT or SIGTERM delivered after `waitLoop` returned; the value `request shutdown` injects into `stopCh` is not one, so after `request shutdown` the operator's first OS signal is the one counted. One goroutine owns `forceCh` from `waitLoop`'s return to exit and reads the phase the hub publishes.

| Phase when the signal arrives | Drain not yet finished | Drain finished, skipped, or aborted |
|-------------------------------|------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Fence (reload worker, confirm window, `commitReloads.close()`) | the drain is marked skipped (log "drain skipped by signal"); the fence continues | force exit 1, as today |
| Drain | the drain context is cancelled (log "drain aborted by signal"); teardown continues, BGP still sends Cease | not reachable |
| Teardown | not reachable | force exit 1, as today |

The line printed on entering each phase states what the next signal does (round 2, N-6; `ai/rules/user-facing-errors.md`):

| Phase entered | Printed line |
|---------------|--------------|
| Fence | "Stopping: finishing config changes (Ctrl+C again to skip the drain)..." |
| Drain, with at least one drain-registered plugin | "Draining routing protocols for up to N s (Ctrl+C again to stop now)..." |
| Teardown, or a drain skipped or aborted | "Shutting down (Ctrl+C again to force)..." |

## Design Insights
- No open-source routing daemon checked drains on SIGTERM (FRR, BIRD, GoBGP, holo, OpenBGPD); commercial NOSes drain through an operator maintenance command. FRR had stop-time OSPF drain in 2005 and removed it for kill in 2023 (research §1.2). The ceiling and the second-signal abort answer why FRR backed away: a supervisor that stops waiting.
- The stop path has more than one Cease producer today: the lifecycle handlers' direct `r.Stop()` (`handleDaemonShutdown`, `handleDaemonQuit`, SSH `stop`) and the plugin exit's `bgpReactor.Stop()`. The drain needs one, the last.
- A per-rail edit is the shape that failed before: `Reactor.Stop`'s comment records that "the rails are found by inspection and the list is never finished". The drain tag therefore rides the egress chains, which the forward rails already run, plus one call in the one originated-route gate (B-3). Round 3 (B-1) found the chains are two, `r.orderedEgressSteps` for `forwardUpdateCore` and `r.egressFilters` for the route server, and that round 2 had read them as one: the step is now built once and placed in both, with its order declared as a stage. Both call sites already sit where provenance is known or can be passed: the forward rails hold the source peer, and the gated writer holds the write's owner and meta. That is why the verdict needs no new rail list, only an explicit provenance at each of its call sites (round 2, B-2; three since round 3: the two forward chains and the gate).
- Round 1 reasoned from the reactor's Adj-RIB-Out, and round 2 found it holds no attributes. The replay source was then chosen by asking which component holds what was sent, with attributes, per peer: only bgp-rib's `ribOut` does (round 2, B-1).

## Known Limitations
- Drain on protocol deletion by commit: Q-5 (a) keeps it out.
- RSVP-TE RFC 5817 graceful shutdown: a conformance gap (Q-7). Its implementation is its own spec, `plan/spec-rsvp-te-graceful-shutdown-rfc5817.md` (owner, 2026-10-13: "make a note to eventually implement RFC 5817 - not urgent").
- The NSSA translator's Type-5 keeps the metric of the Type-7 it translates (Q-8 boundary, confirmed by design review round 9, N-2).
- A drained type 1 external cannot steer onto a type 2 alternate. RFC 2328 Section 16.4 step (6) (b): "Type 1 external paths are always preferred over type 2 external paths." (The review cited this as Section 16.4.1 (b); the sentence is in Section 16.4 step (6), and Section 16.4.1 holds the intra-AS path preferences.) Ze's receiver applies it by rank before metric (`betterExternal`, `routeTypeRank`, `spf/external.go`). Raising a type 1 metric to 0x7fffff moves traffic onto another type 1 path only; a receiver whose alternate is a type 2 external keeps Ze's drained type 1 route until the adjacency drops. Changing the E-bit during the drain would steer onto such an alternate, but it rewrites an attribute the drain otherwise keeps and is not in this design (round 9, I-1).
- The drain values leave room for any realistic distance, not any distance: a receiver more than 0x7fffff from Ze (OSPF type 1) or 0x7f000000 (IS-IS wide) withdraws the drained route, and an alternate costlier than the drain value plus the distance to Ze still loses to it. Both bounds need well over a hundred links at the maximum metric (Data Flow, "Injected prefixes").
- FRR (ospfd and ospf6d, `frr-10.3.1`) keeps a type 1 external at any sum below 2^32: it skips an LSA only when the LSA's own metric is LSInfinity and adds the distance with no cap (`ospf_ase.c` `new->cost = asbr_route->cost + metric`, `ospf6_asbr.c` likewise). The OSPF type 1 bound above, and the choice of 0x7fffff over 0xfffffe, are for Ze's receiver and any receiver that caps the type 1 sum at LSInfinity, not for FRR, and the interop OSPF routing-table checks therefore carry no withdrawing-value red (AC-34, round 10 I-1).
- RFC 8326 receiver side (inbound LOCAL_PREF on received GRACEFUL_SHUTDOWN): not in this spec (Q-4).
- RFC 8326 Section 4 SHOULD ("The LOCAL_PREF value SHOULD be lower than any of the alternative paths"): met at every iBGP receiver by LOCAL_PREF 0 on the tagged copies; Ze's own Loc-RIB keeps the path's LOCAL_PREF, so Ze's own transit traffic keeps the exit until the Cease. Recorded against Section 4, with Section 4.2's inbound bullet as the substituted procedure (Q-10).
- Reflected and route-server routes are not tagged (B-1); their clients lose them at the Cease unless a redundant reflector or route server holds them. This holds for a reflected path whose next hop Ze set to itself (`next-hop self`), whose traffic does cross Ze: tagging it needs LOCAL_PREF 0, which RFC 4456 Section 10 says a reflector SHOULD NOT set (round 2, I-1).
- An admitted commit reload in the config fence waits up to its apply budget (`capBudget`, at most 600 s), so a stop that meets one can exceed `TimeoutStopSec` or gokrazy's 100 s and be killed; the drain and the teardown stay bounded (round 2, I-8).
- Without bgp-rib, routes already advertised are not re-advertised with GRACEFUL_SHUTDOWN; the drain logs the refusal (AC-26).
- Maintenance drain without stop, and its cancel: its own spec, `plan/spec-runtime-drain-and-undrain.md` (Q-2; owner, 2026-10-13: "we want runtime but it need design").
- RFC 8326 Section 4.3 per-neighbour knob ("configuration knobs that utilize the GRACEFUL_SHUTDOWN community to inform BGP neighbors in preparation for an impending neighbor shutdown"): this spec gives the whole-router stop knob only; a knob for one neighbour's maintenance is a gap, designed with the runtime drain in `plan/spec-runtime-drain-and-undrain.md` (round 3, I-7).
- An external plugin that re-announces learned paths with `update text` is classed as an origination at the gate, by construction: its write carries an `originated` owner with no receipt and no peer-up provenance, so Ze cannot tell it from a local origination, and it is tagged as originated (round 3, I-1).
- A drain whose `DrainEventsApplied` returns an error (deadline, a retained earlier delivery failure, or a stopped owner) skips the walk with a line carrying that error (round 4, N-5); paths already advertised then keep their attributes, and paths written during the drain are still tagged (B-2).
