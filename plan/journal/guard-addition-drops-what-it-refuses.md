# Guard addition drops what it refuses

A guard added to enforce one obligation turns an unconditional send into a
conditional one. The refused branch has to do something, and `return` is the
cheapest thing to write. It is also a silent drop, and a drop is usually a
different obligation broken on the same path. Ask what the refused input owed
before you decide what the refusal does with it.

| Date | Spec | Surface | Symptom | Fix |
|------|------|---------|---------|-----|
| 2026-08-24 | fixit-ipsec-peer-reload-ignored | ike engine startup, `applyIPsecConfig` in `internal/component/ike/engine/register.go` | The spec added a REFUSAL: a reload whose `interface` cannot supply an address, while some peer names no `local-address` of its own, must roll the transaction back rather than restart every such peer into a state that cannot bind. Correct, and it was written as a `return` inside the body BOTH deliveries share. Startup reaches that body too, so the early return skipped the IKE transport, the NAT-T transport, the virtual-IP pool, `reconcilePeers` and the active-config store, and `OnConfigure` logged the error and returned nil. A box whose WAN link came up after the daemon then had NO IPsec at all, including for the peers that carry their own `local-address` and cannot be affected by the interface. Before the refusal existed the same configuration warned and served them. The function's own comment and `docs/architecture/ike/ipsec-7-ikev2-engine.md` both stated the opposite behaviour, so the record and the code disagreed from the day the refusal landed | fixed at closure. `applyPhase` carries which delivery is running into the branch, and `unbindablePeers` names the condition as a value rather than a control-flow decision. A reload returns it; startup logs it and applies. The recurrence point past the two rows above: this refusal's dropped branch was not a message but an entire APPLY, and the tell was in the diff all along, a `return` added to a function whose other caller nobody re-read. `test/ipsec/ipsec-startup-serves-bindable-peers.ci` was proven to redden against a faithful simulation of the defect, at `stderr does not contain "ike: started peer"` |
| 2026-08-12 | fixit-ike-request-window | ike engine | the one-request window added for RFC 7296 Section 2.3 made `sendDeleteIKE` and `sendDeleteESP` (`engine/delete.go`) return silently on a held window, where they had sent unconditionally. That broke Section 2.1's enrolled MUST to remember a request until its response arrives, and left the peer holding an SA Ze had torn down. Three reviewer lenses also refused the first two repairs. One was a deferral queue no production caller can drain. The other freed the window before the goodbye, which is the same Section 2.3 violation with local bookkeeping in front of it | `PeerSession.writeDelete` arms `SA.armRequestRetransmit`, so the window's own retransmission carries the Delete. `PeerSession.sayGoodbye` (`engine/established.go`) waits `goodbyeWindowWait` for the outstanding answer instead of abandoning it, and `PeerSession.serviceRequestWindow` deems the SA failed rather than forgetting the request. The queue was deleted |
| 2026-08-23 | - | ddos-detect rolling baseline | `(*baseline).Add` (`internal/plugins/ddos/detect/baseline.go`) returned before appending whenever its `attacking` argument was set, and `applyTick` passes `attacking` OR `above`, where `above` is `ppsAbove` OR `bpsAbove`. So no sample at or above the current threshold could enter the window at all. Against a real flood that is exactly right, and it is the guard that stops an attack raising its own threshold. Against a permanent rise in offered load, a new customer or a migrated service, it latched: `p99Cache` could not rise, `(*baseline).Threshold` could not rise, `above` stayed true, and the detector sat in `stateActive` for good against traffic that is not an attack. Nothing in the package broke the cycle, no timeout, no ceiling on attack duration, no slow-adapt path | `(*baseline).admit` admits one sample per `slowAdaptSamples` (3600) CONSECUTIVE attacking samples, and any sample below the threshold resets the run, so a condition that flaps never accumulates. At the default 1-second check-interval that is one admission per hour, and the p99 of a 300-sample window needs 4 to 13 of them, so a sustained new level is followed after 4 to 13 hours while a shorter flood leaves the number where it was. The damping factor is the whole separation, so both halves are pinned by tests: `TestDetectorAdaptsToASustainedLoadIncrease` reddens when the refusal has no way back, and `TestDetectorHoldsTheThresholdThroughASustainedAttack` reddens when the refusal is simply deleted |
