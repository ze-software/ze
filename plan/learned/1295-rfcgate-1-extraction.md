# 1295 -- rfcgate-1-extraction

## Context

`make ze-rfc-check` was green over 2720 gated MUST-level requirements across 166 enrolled
RFCs, and nothing anywhere bounded what the summaries had MISSED. The gate audits the
requirements a summary LISTS; an obligation nobody wrote down was invisible to it, to all
four ratchets, and to `/ze-rfc-audit`. The only function that ever read an RFC's own text,
`source_keyword_count`, had three consumers and all three were presence-only,
zero-capture-only, or rendering, so once a summary captured one gated MUST the source was
never consulted again for the rest of that summary's life. Re-derived over the corpus, the
hole was large: 4208 capitalised normative sites against 2720 captured requirements, 69
RFCs with more sites than captures, and a raw deficit of 1845. This spec built the
machinery that bounds the miss: a per-RFC extraction sign-off a machine re-checks, gating a
NEW enrolment, grandfathering the rest as a counted and published backlog, ratcheting the
signed count upward, and comparing it against an owner-authored drain policy.

## Decisions

- A per-RFC sign-off artifact under `rfc/extraction/<stem>.json`, over (a) a bare monotonic captured-count ratchet, which `check_retired_requirements` already strictly dominates and which says nothing about what was never extracted, and (b) gating full per-site mapping of all ~4265 sites at once, which would red 166 RFCs on day one for work nobody has done.
- Site granularity with a section axis beside it, over section-only classification: measured, 2862 hole-sites sit in sections that ALREADY carry ids, so section-only would miss 48% of them and would pass RFC 4271 as nearly complete.
- Everything is DERIVED at check time (sites, sections, quotes, register, every published count); only dispositions and reasons are authored. A hand-typed "sites seen" is a claim, and claims are what this program exists to remove (`ai/rules/derive-not-hardcode.md`).
- The REGISTER is derived from the source, and an artifact may claim it or something weaker but never stronger. Chose this over trusting an authored `method` field, because the RFCs that would most benefit from claiming the strong grade are exactly the ones whose source cannot support it.
- Three registers, not one: `rfc2119` (capitalised keywords, at least as many sites as gated rows), `prose` (lowercase indicative modals), `manual-walk` (no mechanical inventory exists). Measured live: 101 / 64 / 1, with **65 of 166** unable to take the strong grade against a spec estimate of 53.
- **Owner ruling 1 (Thomas, 2026-07-29): every register earns drain credit, and every register is published apart.** Chosen over the spec's own earlier design of crediting only `rfc2119`. Excluding weak registers looks strict and is not: it would have left 53 of 166 enrolled RFCs -- including OSPFv2 and RFC 2181, whose Section 3 says it does not use the 2119 expressions -- permanently undrainable. A backlog with no exit is not a stricter ratchet; it is one that gets deleted rather than obeyed. Credit and evidence-strength are two facts and get two columns.
- **Owner ruling 2 (Thomas, 2026-07-29): rfc7296 is re-authored NOW, outside the grandfather.** Two CONFIRMED unextracted MUSTs (`rfc/full/rfc7296.txt:1397` and `:1439`) were read during research. Grandfathering bounds what a NEW MECHANISM may red on; it was never permission to leave a named, verified obligation unextracted once somebody has looked (`ai/rules/rfc-compliance.md:29`, `ai/rules/no-parking.md`).
- **Owner ruling 3 (Thomas, 2026-07-29): the pilot becomes its own spec, and all 108 unimplemented MUSTs are fixed there.** Phase 8a's walk ran as ruling 2 required and returned **214 distinct obligations** against the 18 gated rows the summary holds, triaged 63 implemented-and-testable, 25 implemented-untested, **108 NOT IMPLEMENTED**, 18 uncertain. Thomas ruled twice and the halves compose: OR-A fix all 108 in the spec that owns them; OR-B split phase 8 out so the four machinery children are not serialized behind an IKEv2 compliance workstream. A change of HOME, not of scope -- `plan/learned/1313-rfcgate-1b-rfc7296-pilot.md` even NARROWED the inherited AC-25 by removing its annotation escape.
- Grandfathering is implemented as SCOPE (judge a stem when it has an artifact; demand one only for a stem enrolled since HEAD), never as an allowlist file, so nothing has to be added to a list of exceptions when a stem stops being one.
- The floor COMPARISON lives with the data it reads (`check_drain_floor`); the POLICY it reads (start date, rate) is authored in `rfc/drain-budget.txt` and owned by the umbrella. No rate, date, or cadence is defaulted anywhere in code, and the file's key set is closed so it can never gain a per-stem row.
- The check rides inside the existing `--check`, over adding a verify stage: `stagesForMode` is hand-duplicated across two branches and pinned by a golden, a three-place change with a known drift history.

## Consequences

- The bound is over keyword-visible SITES, not over obligations. Recall is the residual, and it is published rather than claimed away. `unsourced-ids` is how a reviewer records an obligation the extractor cannot see -- RFC 4271 Section 8.2.2 is 35168 characters of the most load-bearing state machine in the product and contains exactly **ONE** capitalised MUST-level keyword. (The research brief said "~45"; that came from slicing to an `8.3.` marker RFC 4271 does not contain, so the slice ran to the end of the document. The corrected figure strengthens the argument: a keyword scan is nearly blind there.)
- Today the bound is ZERO: 166 of 166 enrolled RFCs are unsigned. This is a floor being raised from nothing, not a ceiling being reached, and the ledger says so on every render.
- From now on, enrolling a new RFC costs an extraction sign-off first. That lands as new work on `plan/spec-followup-rfc-enrollment.md` (which owns `rfc/enrolled.txt`) and on `plan/spec-rfcgate-4-ledger.md`, whose D8 enrols four stems -- two of which have no `rfc/full/` source text yet, and with no source there is no inventory to derive and no register to sign under.
- A FIRST sign-off can exclude 100% of its sites and no ratchet sees it: `check_extraction_ratchet` compares a stem against its own HEAD row, and a first sign-off has no baseline. Accepted, not overlooked -- a numeric cap on the exclusion ratio is gamed by rewording and picks a number nobody can defend. The control is publication (the per-RFC exclusion ratio is a ledger column) and the reviewer. The ratchet controls every sign-off after the first.
- A zero-site inventory yields a valid `manual-walk` sign-off that classifies nothing, and the artifact cannot distinguish "the walk found no obligation" from "the derivation found nothing and the reviewer signed the empty result". `rfc1877` is the only live case. The `register-reason` string is the only place that difference can live, so it is load-bearing prose no gate reads.
- The derivation is scoped to stems that HAVE an artifact, and the ledger leaves derived columns blank for unsigned stems. That was forced by measurement, not taste: deriving all 166 costs 1.94s and `check_ledger_fresh` re-renders on every `--check`, which would have been a +76% gate. A fully drained corpus will add ~4.2s to every `--check`; whoever drains it should expect to revisit the memo or the scoping.
- Shipped cost: `--check` +0.07s (+3%), `make ze-rfc-check` ~8.8s to 11.7s end to end. The `--selftest` growth (217 to 301 tests, +2.2s) is almost entirely the live-corpus class, which is the only thing that catches a derivation passing every fixture and returning nothing on real RFC formatting.
- SHOULD/MAY-level sites stay outside the inventory, matching the existing scope decision that advisory levels are listed and may be tagged but never gate.

## Gotchas

- **A skeleton generator that emits a file its own parser refuses bricks the ENTIRE gate, not just that RFC.** `run_check` catches the `ParseError` and prints `cannot run`, so ONE bad artifact hides every other violation in the repository. Four ENROLLED stems were affected -- `rfc1195`, `rfc2865`, `rfc2869`, `sflow-v5` -- because `_SECTION_HEADING_RE` matched column-0 table rows and TOC lines, emitting a duplicate section id; each duplicate body also restarted the per-section site counter, so rfc1195 lost 6 sentences to locator collision, silently, in the direction of green. The comment asserting "body text is indented three spaces so this cannot match prose" was simply false. Those four are the regression fixtures for anyone touching that regex. Fixed by merging a repeated id in `_section_bodies`, asserting locator uniqueness at the producer, and staging + round-tripping every skeleton through the real parser before `os.replace`.
- **A quota whose credit and whose denominator describe different sets can be satisfied without draining anything.** Credit counted every valid sign-off; the backlog counted only enrolled ones. Eleven un-enrolled RFC source texts already sit in the tree as free credit. Measured on 8 enrolled with 6 un-enrolled sign-offs: floor satisfied, backlog still 8 of 8, and the envelope publishing `signed + backlog > enrolled`, a figure no set can have. Every published figure now reads off one `credited()` set.
- **Capping a floor at the RESIDUAL backlog while comparing it to a CUMULATIVE count double-counts every sign-off**, because each one raises the total AND lowers the bar. The condition collapses to `signed >= enrolled/2`: measured, 166 enrolled at 100/month over 12 months flipped green at exactly 83, so no rate the owner could ever arm would demand more than half the corpus. Cap against the DRAINABLE set (the whole enrolled set) instead; self-retirement still holds by a different route, because a drained corpus has `signed == enrolled` and the floor can never exceed `enrolled`.
- **Two tests asserted against the live corpus** -- `extraction_stems() == set()` and the shipped `rate 0` -- so the first real sign-off would have redded one and Thomas's own arming commit the other. That is the exact "a rule that reds the gate on unrelated work gets removed rather than obeyed" failure the machinery exists to prevent, built INTO the machinery. A live-corpus test asserts a census; write the PROPERTY over a fixture tree instead ("no stem is accused for lacking a sign-off it never had"), and keep live-tree classes for questions only the real corpus can answer.
- **`make ze-verify-changed` could not run this diff's own tests, and could not have.** `scripts/dev/changed-pkgs.sh` filtered every git query on `*.go`, so a diff whose entire test surface was a `.py` file selected ZERO packages and scoped verification reported green having tested none of it. The hole was general: any change to a Python tool under `scripts/`, or to the RFC corpus, was invisible to scoped verification. Fixed by adding `*.py` and `rfc` pathspecs mapped to `./scripts/dev`, the one Go package that executes Python.
- **Mutation testing found what 252 (then 295) passing tests did not.** Every derived number in the published table could be zeroed while green, because `signed` was defined as `sum(counts.values())`, making "the keys sum to the total" a tautology no test could ever fail; two derivations that must agree is a real cross-check and a definition is not. Separately, the sole load-bearing line with no test at all was `errs.extend(parse_errs)` in `run_check` -- delete it and every test stayed green while an unparseable ENROLLED summary exited 0 with `evaluate` seeing zero requirements, i.e. every MUST of that RFC silently stopped being checked while the gate printed OK.
- **A site quoting a capitalised MUST could be mapped to a SHOULD row and reported as captured**, while `evaluate` never gates a SHOULD -- the RFC's own MUST downgraded to advice inside the artifact whose entire purpose is to bound what the summary understated. Both facts were already present and simply were not compared.
- **Recording a correction is not applying it.** Two spec corrections were written into a Phase Results table and then applied at NONE of the sites they named. One of them, an unrunnable `make ze-rfc-extraction-status --json` (GNU make parses `--json` as its own option and exits 2 before any recipe runs), stood at eleven sites across two specs -- including the two Deliverables rows instructing this spec's own closer to run it and record the output as verification.
- **A `resign-reason` carried forward automatically is not a justification.** `_artifact_document` copies the field on every refresh, so once set it is permanently non-empty and a presence test guards nothing: exclusions could climb indefinitely behind one sentence written years earlier, with only the date to edit. The check now demands the reason CHANGE for each rise.
- **`float("nan")` passes every ordinary guard**, because NaN compares false against every operator, and it reached `math.ceil` outside `run_check`'s `try` -- a traceback where the spec required a clean exit 2. Refuse non-finite values where every other malformed value is refused.
- **A memo keyed on a stem alone hands a second body the first body's inventory**, silently and toward green. The key must fingerprint the RAW bytes, not the normalized ones: `_normalize` strips lines and drops blanks, and the derivation depends on exactly those (`_SECTION_HEADING_RE` anchors at `^`, `_sentences` splits on blank lines), so two bodies differing only in indentation share one normalized sha and derive different section sets.
- **Both owner questions had the same shape, and both were answered against the cautious-looking option.** Excluding weak registers LOOKED strict and would have created a third of a corpus that could never be drained; leaving rfc7296 to the backlog LOOKED consistent with grandfathering and would have left two confirmed obligations unextracted. Each time, the conservative move was the one that quietly reduced what Ze owes, which is the direction the rules never permit. Grandfathering and no-parking do not actually conflict: grandfathering bounds what a NEW MECHANISM may red on, no-parking binds what a PERSON who has read an obligation may do next. The apparent tension came from reading a scope rule as an entitlement.
