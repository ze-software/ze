# Test weakenings this commit accepts

Nothing left the suite. One helper, `alertRound`, is replaced by `alertFlight`
in the same file, and every test that called it calls the replacement.

`alertRound` drove `tlsMethod.Process` directly and stopped on `m.alertSent`,
the field this commit deletes. It could not survive the change: the cause is no
longer parked on the method, so there is no method-level flag left to stop on.
`alertFlight` drives the real `eap.Session` against the real peer instead and
stops on `Session.Err`, which is where the cause is now recorded. It stops one
round EARLIER than `alertRound` did, at the packet that carries the fatal TLS
alert, and it never hands that packet to the peer. That is the point of the
rewrite: it is the round sequence a peer that abandons the exchange produces,
which is what strongSwan does and what `alertRound` could not express.

The three tests that used it each keep their assertions and gain one.
`TestEAPTLSAuthenticatorSendsTheAlertBeforeItReportsTheFailure` still reads the
alert packet's code, its L flag, its declared length and its TLS record content
type, and now also reads the cause on that same round and the EAP-Failure that
answers the peer's reply. `TestEAPTLSRejectedPeerCannotSteerTheReportedCause`
still feeds both usurping replies and now asserts the EAP-Failure they draw as
well. `TestEAPTLS13RecordsTheRevocationWhenTheRefusedPeerWalksAway` is new.

Evidence: `go test ./internal/core/eap/` passes, and each of the three tests
was observed RED under a break of the code its claim rests on. The two tagged
claims carry recorded proofs in `rfc/discrimination/rfc5216.json`
(`RFC5216-2.1.3-3 positive` under a broken `Session.finalRequest`, and
`RFC5216-2.1.3-4 positive` under a broken `Session.failure`).

| Test | Reason |
|------|--------|
